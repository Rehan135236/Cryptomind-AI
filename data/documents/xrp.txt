XRP is the native digital asset of the XRP Ledger (XRPL), an open-source, public, decentralized blockchain created in 2012 by David Schwartz, Jed McCaleb, and Arthur Britto to facilitate fast, low-cost cross-border payments and currency transfers.

Unlike Bitcoin or Ethereum, the XRP Ledger does not use Proof-of-Work or Proof-of-Stake consensus. Instead, it employs the XRP Ledger Consensus Protocol, a Federated Byzantine Agreement (FBA) network. A set of independent validating nodes (Unique Node Lists or UNLs) aggregate and reach agreement on transaction ordering every 3 to 5 seconds.

XRP functions as a bridge currency for cross-border liquidity and fiat currency settlement, allowing financial institutions and users to transfer value internationally in seconds without pre-funding nostro/vostro accounts.

The total maximum supply of XRP is capped at 100 billion tokens, all created at ledger inception. A significant portion of XRP supply is held in cryptographic escrow accounts by Ripple Labs, released periodically according to pre-programmed schedules.

XRPL transactions require a small fractional burn of XRP as a transaction fee (destroying a fraction of a drop, where 1 XRP = 1,000,000 drops) to prevent transaction spamming.

XRPL addresses use Base58 encoding (starting with 'r') with cryptographic key pairs. Destination tags are frequently used to route payments on centralized exchanges.

Key risks and limitations include historical legal disputes regarding regulatory classification as a security, reliance on default UNL lists for network consensus coordination, and concentrated token holdings in corporate escrow accounts.
