Source: Avalanche Consensus Whitepaper by Emin Gün Sirer et al. (Ava Labs) and Official Avalanche Documentation.

Avalanche is a smart contract platform designed for high throughput, sub-second finality, and custom blockchain network (subnet) deployments, created in 2020 by Emin Gün Sirer and Ava Labs.

Avalanche utilizes a novel consensus protocol family based on repeated random subsampling. Instead of polling all validators, nodes repeatedly sample small random subsets of validators to reach rapid network agreement in under 1 second.

The Avalanche Primary Network comprises three built-in blockchains:
1. X-Chain (Exchange Chain): Manages asset creation and exchange using a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) UTXO model.
2. P-Chain (Platform Chain): Coordinates validators, manages staking, and enables creation of custom application-specific subnets.
3. C-Chain (Contract Chain): Executes Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM) smart contracts using an account model.

AVAX is the native utility token of Avalanche. It is used to pay transaction gas fees across the primary network, secure the network via Proof-of-Stake validator and delegator staking, and serve as a common accounting unit for subnets.

AVAX has a maximum capped supply of 720 million AVAX. Half of the supply (360 million) was minted at launch, while the remainder is issued as staking rewards. All transaction fees across X-Chain, P-Chain, and C-Chain are burned, creating deflationary pressure during high usage.

C-Chain uses standard Ethereum-compatible 0x addresses, while X-Chain and P-Chain use Avalanche-formatted Bech32 addresses.

Key risks and limitations include complex multi-chain architecture maintenance, minimum validator staking requirement thresholds (2,000 AVAX), and competition among L1 and L2 scaling solutions.
