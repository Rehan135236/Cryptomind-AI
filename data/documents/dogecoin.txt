Dogecoin (DOGE) is an open-source peer-to-peer cryptocurrency created in December 2013 by Billy Markus and Jackson Palmer as a lighthearted, meme-inspired digital currency based on the "Doge" Shiba Inu internet meme.

Dogecoin was originally created as a fork of Luckycoin, which itself was a fork of Litecoin. Dogecoin operates on its own dedicated layer-1 blockchain utilizing the Scrypt hashing algorithm for Proof-of-Work (PoW) consensus.

In 2014, Dogecoin implemented Auxiliary Proof-of-Work (AuxPoW), enabling merged mining with Litecoin. This allows Litecoin miners to mine Dogecoin simultaneously without consuming additional computational energy, securing the Dogecoin network.

DOGE is primarily used as a tipping system, digital micro-payment currency, and peer-to-peer medium of exchange due to its fast 1-minute block confirmation times and negligible transaction fees.

Unlike Bitcoin, Dogecoin has no hard cap on its total supply. The network emits a fixed block subsidy of 10,000 DOGE per block, resulting in a predictable annual inflation of 5 billion DOGE. This design encourages spending rather than long-term speculative hoarding.

Dogecoin transactions require low network fees paid in DOGE. Dogecoin addresses start with the letter 'D' and use standard public/private key pairs.

Key risks and limitations include an infinite inflationary supply model, lack of native smart contract functionality, vulnerability to speculative sentiment and social media volatility, and developer maintenance dependence.
