Litecoin (LTC) is an open-source peer-to-peer cryptocurrency created in October 2011 by Charlie Lee, a former Google engineer. Designed as a lightweight fork of the Bitcoin codebase, Litecoin was created to serve as a fast, low-cost medium for daily global payments—often referred to as "the silver to Bitcoin's gold."

Litecoin shares Bitcoin's fundamental architecture but incorporates key technical modifications:
1. Block Time: Litecoin produces blocks every 2.5 minutes (four times faster than Bitcoin's 10-minute target), enabling faster transaction confirmation times.
2. Hashing Algorithm: Litecoin uses the Scrypt hashing algorithm for Proof-of-Work (PoW) consensus, which was originally memory-intensive to prevent ASIC dominance before dedicated Scrypt ASICs emerged.
3. Supply Cap: Total maximum supply is capped at 84 million LTC (four times Bitcoin's 21 million limit).

Like Bitcoin, Litecoin emission follows a halving schedule. Every 840,000 blocks (roughly every four years), the miner block reward is halved until the 84 million cap is reached.

In 2022, Litecoin activated MimbleWimble Extension Blocks (MWEB), an optional privacy upgrade allowing users to perform confidential transactions that conceal transfer amounts and wallet balances.

Litecoin addresses use Base58 or Bech32 encoding ('L...', 'M...', or 'ltc1...'). Transactions require low network fees paid in LTC.

Key risks and limitations include regulatory scrutiny over MWEB privacy features (resulting in delistings on select strict regional exchanges), competition from Layer 2 payment networks, and reliance on Proof-of-Work mining security.
